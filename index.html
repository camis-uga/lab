Research

Research Projects

Roadway Infrastructure Readiness for Automated Driving

Evaluating roadway infrastructure from the automated-driving perspective to determine how reliably automated driving systems can operate across different road classes, infrastructure features, and roadway conditions.

View Project on GitHub →

CrashAlign: End-to-End Crash Detection and Understanding

An end-to-end framework for recognizing crashes in traffic videos, locating when and where they occur, and interpreting what happened and why.

View Project on GitHub →

Intersection Risk Evaluation Based on LiDAR Data

From LiDAR-based vehicle detection and tracking we recover near-crash events and learn where and how risk may occur.

View Project on GitHub →

AnchorReasoning: Visually Grounded Reasoning for End-to-End Driving

A visually grounded chain-of-thought dataset built on WOD-E2E, with 416K frames and 395K decision-critical elements, that teaches vision-language driving models to link what they see to why they act and how they plan.

View Project on GitHub →

GRIP-Fusion: Roadside Camera–LiDAR Perception for Intersection Safety

Sponsored by GDOT, this project collects a new roadside intersection dataset with LiDAR, camera and traffic-conflict labels, and develops GRIP-Fusion, a one-LiDAR + one-camera 3D detector that achieves state-of-the-art results on three public benchmarks.

View Project on GitHub →

EgoManeuver: Fine-Grained Ego-Vehicle Action Recognition

A benchmark that unifies seven public driving datasets (86.6K segments, 190.8 hours of video) into 16 lateral and 6 longitudinal ego-vehicle actions, with the DenseEgo baseline and an agent that retrieves driving video by maneuver.

View Project on GitHub →

PACE-ADS: Psychology and Cognition Enabled Automated Driving

A framework that personalizes automated driving to the occupant: a driver agent reads the traffic scene, a psychologist agent infers occupant state from EEG, heart rate, facial expression and verbal commands, and a coordinator selects and explains the driving behavior, tested in four closed-loop CARLA scenarios.

View Project on GitHub →

Probing LLMs for Autonomous Driving Behavior

A study of how GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3 and Llama 3.1 choose high-level driving actions in four everyday scenarios, each with 1,500 contextual variants in three prompt languages, using an ordered logit model and thematic analysis of the models' justifications.

View Project on GitHub →

Sample-and-Verify: Test-Time Plan Selection for Vision-Language Driving Models

Greedy decoding hides good plans. Sample-and-verify keeps a fine-tuned vision-language planner frozen, adds temperature samples to its greedy plan, and lets a small verifier trained only on logged futures pick the plan to execute, evaluated on WOD-E2E and NAVSIM.

View Project on GitHub →

StuckSolver: LLM-Assisted Recovery of Immobilized Autonomous Vehicles

A plug-in LLM agent that detects when an autonomous vehicle is stuck, reasons about the cause from camera images and perception data, and issues a recovery plan on its own or with passenger guidance. It raises the Bench2Drive driving score of a rule-based CARLA agent from 48.7 to 65.2, and to 70.9 with passenger guidance.

View Paper →

Joint optimization of mixed wireless charging facility placement and electric bus charging operations

A mixed optimization model jointly plans WCS/WCL placement and bus charging profiles. Heterogeneous charging rates vary by facility type, road class, and SoC.

View Paper →

Evaluating Speed Reduction Techniques in Construction Zones to Improve Safety

Sponsored by GDOT and FHWA, this study evaluates traditional and innovative speed reduction techniques in Georgia construction work zones to provide evidence-based recommendations that reduce crashes, injuries and fatalities among workers and the motoring public.

View Project Page →

Principal Investigator

Qianwen (Cami) Li, Ph.D.

Dr. Li is an Assistant Professor at the School of Environmental, Civil, Agricultural and Mechanical Engineering (ECAM) at the University of Georgia. She is the Director of the Georgia Network for Electric Mobility - Future Mobility Living Lab . Her research interests include Connected, Automated, Electric, and Shared Mobility; Smart Infrastructure Systems; and Traffic Flow Theory.
